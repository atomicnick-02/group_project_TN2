\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Swinging an underactuated double pendulum from its stable hanging configuration up
to an unstable equilibrium --- and then \emph{holding} it there --- is a classic
benchmark in nonlinear control. The task is hard for two reasons: the plant is
strongly nonlinear and torque-limited, so the swing-up must pump energy through a
carefully timed sequence of motions; and the upright goal is an unstable
equilibrium, so balancing demands fast, accurate feedback. Reinforcement learning
can solve such tasks but is sample-hungry and delicate to tune. This project takes
a different route: \emph{imitation learning}, in which a classical optimal-control
expert is constructed first, demonstrations are harvested from it, and a generative
neural policy is fit to those demonstrations.

This report documents four Python modules that together implement that pipeline
end to end for a system-identified double pendulum. The pipeline proceeds in four
stages: (i) \file{kux\_generation.py} solves a family of direct-collocation
trajectory-optimization problems and computes time-varying LQR (TVLQR) feedback
gains about each optimal trajectory; (ii) \file{generate\_tvlqr\_dataset\_kux.py}
rolls those TVLQR controllers out in closed loop through the identified plant to
synthesise an expert demonstration dataset; (iii) \file{diffusion\_policy.py}
defines a conditional Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) over action
sequences with both MLP and Transformer backbones; and (iv)
\file{train\_diffusion\_policy.py} trains that diffusion policy by behavioural
cloning on the expert dataset.

The remainder of the report is organised as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:overview} gives a high-level overview of the pipeline and the
file-on-disk interfaces that decouple its stages, and Section~\ref{sec:plant}
specifies the shared plant model. Sections~\ref{sec:kux}--\ref{sec:train} then
describe the four stages in turn: the optimal-control expert and its TVLQR
stabilisation, the closed-loop dataset generation, the diffusion-policy model, and
its training by behavioural cloning. Section~\ref{sec:tricks} isolates the
modifications that adapt a textbook DDPM into a closed-loop controller for an
unstable, torque-limited plant. Finally, Section~\ref{sec:interfaces} collects the
data contracts between stages, Section~\ref{sec:limits} records the known
limitations of the resulting controller, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}
concludes.

\section{Overview of the Pipeline}
\label{sec:overview}

The goal is to learn a neural feedback policy that swings a double pendulum from
the hanging-down configuration up to an unstable equilibrium (upright, or the
folded $[\pi,\pi]$ pose) and holds it there. Rather than learning from scratch
with reinforcement learning, the project uses \emph{imitation learning}: a
classical optimal-control expert is constructed, demonstrations are harvested
from it, and a generative policy is fit to those demonstrations.

\section{The Plant Model}
\label{sec:plant}

All four stages share one plant: the system-identified ``cloudpendulum'' model.
\file{kux\_generation.py} encodes it directly; the dataset generator imports the
same parameters \code{P} from \file{rollout\_tvlqr.py}. The continuous dynamics
follow the standard manipulator equation
\begin{equation}
M(x)\,\ddot q \;+\; C(x)\,\dot q \;+\; f(\dot q) \;=\; u \;+\; G(x),
\label{eq:manip}
\end{equation}
with $q = [q_1,q_2]^\top$. The mass matrix, Coriolis matrix and gravity vector are
\begin{align}
M(x) &=
\begin{bmatrix}
I_1+I_2+l_1^2 m_2 + 2 l_1 m_2 l_{c2}\cos q_2 + g_{r1}^2 I_r + I_r &\; I_2 + l_1 m_2 l_{c2}\cos q_2\\
I_2 + l_1 m_2 l_{c2}\cos q_2 &\; I_2
\end{bmatrix},\\[4pt]
C(x) &=
\begin{bmatrix}
-2\dot q_2\,k\sin q_2 & -\dot q_2\,k\sin q_2\\
\phantom{-}\dot q_1\,k\sin q_2 & 0
\end{bmatrix},\quad k = l_1 m_2 l_{c2},\\[4pt]
G(x) &=
\begin{bmatrix}
-g\,m_1 l_{c1}\sin q_1 - g\,m_2(l_1\sin q_1 + l_{c2}\sin(q_1+q_2))\\
-g\,m_2 l_{c2}\sin(q_1+q_2)
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{align}
Friction is a viscous term plus an $\arctan$-smoothed Coulomb term (the smooth
surrogate for $\mathrm{sign}(\dot q)$, with \code{FRICTION\_SCALE}$=100$):
\begin{equation}
f(\dot q) = \begin{bmatrix} b_1\dot q_1 + \mu_1\arctan(100\,\dot q_1)\\
b_2\dot q_2 + \mu_2\arctan(100\,\dot q_2)\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Solving \eqref{eq:manip} for the accelerations gives the first-order field
$\dot x = f(x,u) = [\dot q,\; M(x)^{-1}(u + G(x) - C(x)\dot q - f(\dot q))]$,
implemented in \code{dynamics(x,u)} and integrated with a fixed-step RK4
\code{rk4\_step}. The identified parameters are listed in Table~\ref{tab:params}.

\paragraph{Symmetry.} The plant is invariant under the left--right reflection
$x \mapsto -x$, $u \mapsto -u$: because the kinematics enter only through
$\sin/\cos$ and friction is odd in velocity, $f(-x,-u) = -f(x,u)$. Hence any
valid trajectory $(x(t),u(t))$ has a mirror twin $(-x(t),-u(t))$ that swings up
to the \emph{same} goal from the other side, and the TVLQR gains $K$ are
identical at the mirrored point. The dataset generator exploits this to double
its demonstrations for free (Section~\ref{sec:dataset}).

\section{Stage 1 --- Trajectory Optimization and TVLQR (\file{kux\_generation.py})}
\label{sec:kux}

For each cost configuration this module (1) solves a direct-collocation
trajectory-optimization problem with IPOPT to obtain the optimal
state/control trajectories $(x,u)$, then (2) linearises about that trajectory and
runs a backward Riccati pass to obtain the time-varying feedback gains $K$.

\subsection{The optimization problem}
With horizon $T = 4$ s and step $dt = 0.05$ s, the trajectory is discretised into
$N = T/dt + 1 = 81$ nodes. The decision vector stacks all states then all
controls,
\[
z = [\,x_0,\dots,x_{N-1},\;u_0,\dots,u_{N-1}\,] \in \mathbb{R}^{N n_x + N n_u},
\qquad n_x = 4,\; n_u = 2.
\]
The cost is a quadratic running cost with a separate terminal weight,
\begin{equation}
J(z) = \tfrac12\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\Big[(x_k - x_g)^\top Q (x_k - x_g) + u_k^\top R u_k\Big]
\;+\; (x_{N-1}-x_g)^\top Q_{\text{fin}} (x_{N-1}-x_g),
\end{equation}
implemented in \code{total\_objective}; its closed-form gradient
\code{total\_objective\_deriv} is supplied to IPOPT analytically (the per-node
gradients $2Q(x_k-x_g)$, $2Ru_k$ are computed with \code{jax.vmap}). The dynamics
are imposed as equality constraints by \emph{trapezoidal collocation} between
neighbouring nodes,
\begin{equation}
x_{k+1} - x_k - \tfrac{dt}{2}\big(f(x_k,u_k) + f(x_{k+1},u_{k+1})\big) = 0,
\label{eq:trap}
\end{equation}
together with the boundary constraints $x_0 = x_0^{\text{start}}$ and
$x_{N-1} = x_g$. The full constraint vector and its Jacobian (via
\code{jax.jacobian}) are assembled in \code{constraints}. Controls are box-bounded
to $\pm\tau_{\max}$; states are free.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Trapezoidal-collocation defect (the dynamics
constraint between two nodes).},captionpos=b]
def trapezoidal_collocation(x_k, x_kp1, u_k, u_kp1, dynamics, dt):
    f_k   = dynamics(x_k,   u_k)
    f_kp1 = dynamics(x_kp1, u_kp1)
    return (x_kp1 - x_k - (dt / 2.0) * (f_k + f_kp1)).flatten()
\end{lstlisting}

The \code{Problem} class bakes a specific $(Q,R,Q_{\text{fin}},x_0,x_g)$ into
JIT-compiled JAX callbacks (\code{objective}, \code{gradient}, \code{constraints},
\code{jacobian}) that conform to the \code{cyipopt.Problem} interface. A linear
interpolation $x_0 \!\to\! x_g$ with zero controls warm-starts the solver
(\code{solve\_trajectory}). IPOPT runs with \code{max\_iter}$=100$, \code{tol}$=10^{-3}$.

\subsection{Time-varying LQR gains}
Stabilising the optimal trajectory requires feedback. The module discretises the
linearised dynamics \emph{through the RK4 integrator} by differentiating each RK4
stage with the chain rule (\code{get\_discrete\_matrices}), producing the
discrete sensitivity matrices $A_d, B_d$ at every node --- this is more faithful
than an Euler discretisation of the continuous Jacobians $A,B$ obtained from
\code{jax.jacfwd}. The finite-horizon discrete Riccati recursion is then swept
backward from the terminal cost $P_{N-1} = Q_{\text{fin}}$:
\begin{align}
S_k   &= R + B_d^\top P_{k+1} B_d, \\
K_k   &= S_k^{-1} B_d^\top P_{k+1} A_d, \\
P_k   &= Q + A_d^\top P_{k+1} (A_d - B_d K_k).
\end{align}
The backward sweep is vectorised with \code{jax.lax.scan} over the reversed
trajectory (\code{compute\_tvlqr\_gains}), yielding gains $K \in
\mathbb{R}^{(N-1)\times n_u \times n_x}$.

\subsection{Outputs}
Each configuration is written to its own folder named by the $Q$/$R$ diagonals
and the goal (e.g.\ \texttt{Q\_100\_100\_100\_100\_\_R\_0p01\_0p01\_\_xgoal\_3p142\_0\_0\_0/}),
containing \texttt{trajectory.csv} ($x$), \texttt{inputs.csv} ($u$),
\texttt{K\_matrix.npy} ($K$), and \texttt{config.json} (metadata, including the
IPOPT status and the achieved final state). The configuration sweep fixes
$Q = \mathrm{diag}(100,100,100,100)$ and sweeps the control weight
$R \in \{0.01,\,10,\,50,\,100,\,200,\,500\}\cdot I_2$ across two goals
$x_g \in \{[\pi,0,0,0],\,[\pi,\pi,0,0]\}$ --- twelve references in total.

\section{Stage 2 --- Expert Dataset Generation (\file{generate\_tvlqr\_dataset\_kux.py})}
\label{sec:dataset}

This module turns the open-loop references of Stage~1 into a behavioural-cloning
dataset by \emph{rolling out} the TVLQR controllers in closed loop through the
identified plant and logging the $(\text{observed state},\text{commanded torque})$
stream at the control rate $dt_{\text{ctrl}} = 0.05$ s. Because every logged pair
is produced by simulating the actual plant, each sample is dynamically valid by
construction.

\subsection{Reference discovery and the hold gain}
\code{discover\_configs} scans \texttt{optimal\_trajectories/} for every folder
holding the full quartet (\texttt{trajectory.csv}, \texttt{inputs.csv},
\texttt{K\_matrix.npy}, \texttt{config.json}); \code{load\_reference} reads back
$(x_{\text{ref}}, u_{\text{ref}}, K, Q, R, x_g)$.

A subtlety: the saved $K$ is the \emph{finite-horizon} TVLQR gain, which ends
with the swing-up and cannot hold the unstable inverted goal past the last node.
The generator therefore solves the \emph{infinite-horizon} LQR at the goal once,
\code{K\_hold = lqr\_gain(xgoal, Q, R, dt=dt\_sim)}, and hands it to every rollout
so the upright hold persists. By the plant symmetry the same $K_{\text{hold}}$
stabilises the mirrored goal $-x_g$. Critically the hold gain is designed for the
\emph{inner simulation step} $dt_{\text{sim}}$, not the control step --- the
inverted hold is not stabilisable at the 20\,Hz control rate, so the rollout
re-closes the hold loop at $dt_{\text{sim}}$.

\subsection{Two integration rates}
The references are 41-node, $0.05$ s trajectories whose swing-ups are violent
($\dot q_2 > 40$ rad/s). Integrating the closed loop at the full $0.05$ s control
step destabilises tracking, so the rollout sub-steps RK4 at
$dt_{\text{sim}} = 0.005$ s (the \code{--dt-sim} flag) while still commanding and
\emph{logging} actions at the $0.05$ s control rate. This separation of the
control rate from the integration rate is what makes the otherwise-fragile
references trackable.

\subsection{DAgger-style noise, mirroring, and hold rollouts}
Three mechanisms broaden the state coverage of the demonstrations:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item[DAgger noise.] On a fraction of swing-up rollouts (every $\sim\!2/3$ of
side-pairs) state noise of std $0.05$ is injected for coverage of off-nominal
states, but the \emph{clean} commanded torque is logged as the supervised target.
The noise latches \emph{off} once the rollout first reaches the goal
(\code{noise\_until\_upright=True}) so the fragile hold stays clean.
\item[Mirroring.] Each reference is also rolled out mirrored, $(-x_{\text{ref}},
-u_{\text{ref}})$, swinging up to the same goal from the other side. Episodes
alternate sides ($+1/-1$) so the kept set is left--right balanced; each rollout
records its \code{side} attribute.
\item[Goal-hold rollouts.] In \code{mode=both}, additional episodes start
\emph{perturbed} around the goal equilibrium and roll out the stabilising gain,
recording the deviation\,$\rightarrow$\,corrective-torque map. Without these the
policy would only ever see the exact fixed point and could never learn to recover
from small deviations.
\end{description}
Every reference (and its mirror) is self-checked --- it must still swing up and
hold in the plant model --- before use; a reference that fails is skipped with a
warning rather than aborting the whole sweep.

\subsection{Output format}
A single HDF5 file is written (e.g.\
\texttt{expert\_trajectories\_kux\_swingup\_hold\_mirrored.h5}) with one group
\texttt{traj\_\{idx\}} per kept rollout. Group indices are global across all
configurations. Each group holds \texttt{states (T,4)} and \texttt{actions (T,2)},
plus per-group attributes that record the expert identity: $Q$, $R$, $x_{\text{goal}}$,
the source config folder name, the initial state, the injected noise std, the
\code{side}, and the \code{kind} (\texttt{swingup}/\texttt{hold}). This is exactly
the format the trainer consumes, and the $x_{\text{goal}}$ attribute is what later
enables (in principle) goal-conditioning.

\section{Stage 3 --- The Diffusion Policy Model (\file{diffusion\_policy.py})}
\label{sec:model}

The policy is a conditional DDPM over short action sequences, after
Chi et al.\ (2023). It \emph{denoises} a sampled Gaussian into an action chunk
$a \in \mathbb{R}^{H\times n_u}$, conditioned on a clean (un-noised) context
vector. The module provides a scheduler, two interchangeable backbones, and the
training/sampling logic.

\subsection{Forward process and the scheduler}
\code{Scheduler} uses a linear $\beta$ schedule from $\beta_0 = 3\!\times\!10^{-4}$
to $\beta_{\!T} = 0.03$ over $T = 100$ steps, with $\alpha_t = 1-\beta_t$ and
$\bar\alpha_t = \prod_{s\le t}\alpha_s$. The forward (noising) process samples
\begin{equation}
q(x_t\mid x_0) = \mathcal N\!\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0,\;(1-\bar\alpha_t) I\big),
\qquad
x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\varepsilon,\;\;\varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I),
\end{equation}
returning both $x_t$ and the noise $\varepsilon$ (\code{add\_noise}). Only the
\emph{actions} are noised; the conditioning context is never noised.

\subsection{Noise-prediction backbones}
Both backbones share the contract
\code{forward(a\_noisy, t, cond) -> predicted\_noise}, so they are drop-in
replacements.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
\item[\code{MLP}.] Flattens the action chunk to $H\!\cdot\!n_u$, embeds the scalar
timestep and the conditioning vector with small SiLU MLPs, concatenates, and maps
through a 3-hidden-layer SiLU network back to $H\!\cdot\!n_u$. It ignores the
temporal structure of the action chunk.
\item[\code{TrajectoryTransformer}.] Treats the $H$ action steps as a sequence of
tokens with learned positional embeddings, and \emph{prepends} two context tokens
--- a sinusoidal timestep embedding and the conditioning embedding --- so every
action token attends to them. A pre-norm \code{TransformerEncoder} (GELU)
processes the length-$(H{+}2)$ sequence; only the $H$ action positions are
projected back to $n_u$. This preserves the temporal structure the MLP discards.
\end{description}

\subsection{The \code{DiffusionPolicy} class}
This wraps a scheduler and a backbone and exposes training, sampling, and a
control-convenience method.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
\item[\code{train}.] For each batch it draws $t\sim\mathcal U\{1,\dots,T\}$, noises
the actions, and minimises the noise-prediction MSE
$\|\varepsilon - \varepsilon_\theta(x_t,t,\text{cond})\|^2$. It uses Adam with a
per-step cosine-annealing LR schedule, automatic mixed precision on CUDA, and
maintains an \textbf{exponential moving average} (EMA, decay $0.999$) of the
weights.
\item[\code{sample}.] Runs reverse DDPM ancestral sampling for one action chunk
per conditioning vector. The flag \code{stochastic=False} drops the per-step
ancestral noise (returns the posterior mean each step), which sharply lowers
action variance --- important for stabilising the unstable upright hold, where
injected sampling noise would perturb the torque and topple the pendulum.
\item[\code{act}.] A closed-loop convenience wrapper: it takes the last $k$
observed states (and optional goal), builds the conditioning vector, samples a
chunk, and returns the first \code{n\_exec} actions.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Why EMA for control.} The EMA copy is a smoother estimate of the
trained function than the last-step weights. Near the unstable upright
equilibrium this yields a more \emph{consistent} learned gain (less jitter), which
is what lets the policy balance. The trainer saves the EMA weights as the
inference checkpoint.

\section{Stage 4 --- Training the Policy (\file{train\_diffusion\_policy.py})}
\label{sec:train}

This module loads the HDF5 expert dataset, converts it into supervised
$(\text{cond}, \text{action\_seq})$ windows, and trains the diffusion policy.

\subsection{Feature engineering and conditioning}
Raw angles are mapped to wrap-free angular features to avoid the
$2\pi$ discontinuity, and velocities/actions are min--max normalised to $[-1,1]$
(stats saved to \texttt{norm\_stats.json} for inference):
\begin{equation}
x \mapsto \big[\sin q_1,\ \cos q_1,\ \sin q_2,\ \cos q_2,\ \hat{\dot q}_1,\ \hat{\dot q}_2\big]
\in \mathbb{R}^{6}\quad(\code{NX\_FEAT}=6).
\end{equation}
For every time index $i$ in a trajectory the dataset forms a conditioning vector
from the last $k = 6$ feature vectors (left-padded at the start) and, when
\code{USE\_GOAL=True}, appends the per-trajectory goal feature read from the HDF5
\texttt{xgoal} attribute:
\[
\text{cond} = \big[\text{hist}_{i-k+1:i}\big]\ \Vert\ \text{goal\_feat}
\;\in\;\mathbb{R}^{k\cdot 6\,(+\,6)}.
\]
The target is the next $H = 10$ normalised actions (right-padded by repetition at
the trajectory's end).

\subsection{Architecture selection and checkpointing}
The architecture is CLI-selectable (\code{--arch mlp|transformer}, default
\code{transformer}) and \textbf{self-describing in the checkpoint}: the network
class name and its exact kwargs are stored under \code{config["net\_kwargs"]}, so
the evaluation script can rebuild the identical network with no manual edits. The
checkpoint stores both the EMA weights (\code{model\_state}, used for inference)
and the raw weights (\code{model\_state\_raw}), along with all conditioning
metadata ($k$, $n_x$, \code{nx\_feat}, \code{use\_goal}, horizon, timesteps).
Periodic per-epoch snapshots are written every \code{CKPT\_EVERY}$=20$ epochs.

\section{Deviations from Vanilla Diffusion: The Implemented Tricks}
\label{sec:tricks}

A textbook DDPM (Ho et al.\ 2020) is \emph{unconditional}: it samples
$t\sim\mathcal U\{1,\dots,T\}$, noises the data $x_0$, trains a network to predict
the noise under MSE, and generates by ancestral reverse sampling from
$\mathcal N(0,I)$. The pipeline keeps that backbone but layers a number of
modifications that adapt it from image-style generation to \emph{closed-loop
control of an unstable, torque-limited plant}. Table~\ref{tab:tricks} summarises
them; the subsections below explain each and point to where it lives in the code.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Vanilla DDPM vs.\ the control-oriented diffusion policy implemented here.}
\label{tab:tricks}
\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{4.3cm} L{5.0cm} L{5.0cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Aspect} & \textbf{Vanilla DDPM} & \textbf{This implementation} \\
\midrule
What is diffused & the whole data sample (e.g.\ an image), unconditional &
an \emph{action chunk} $a\in\mathbb R^{H\times n_u}$, conditioned on clean state
history $+$ goal \\
Diffusion length & $T\approx 1000$, $\beta\!\in[10^{-4},2\!\times\!10^{-2}]$ &
short $T=100$, tuned $\beta\!\in[3\!\times\!10^{-4},3\!\times\!10^{-2}]$ \\
Timestep input & integer index $t$ & normalized scalar $t/T\in(0,1)$ \\
Inference weights & last-step weights & EMA weights (decay $0.999$) \\
Reverse sampling & always stochastic (ancestral noise) &
optional \emph{deterministic} (posterior mean) for the unstable hold \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\subsection{Reformulation: what is actually diffused}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
\item[Conditional, action-only diffusion.] Only the actions are noised; the
conditioning context (state history $+$ goal) is passed \emph{clean} and is never
corrupted (\code{train}: \code{z\_t, eps = scheduler.add\_noise(action\_seq, t)};
\code{cond} bypasses the scheduler). This turns the generative model into a policy
$p_\theta(a\mid \text{cond})$ rather than an unconditional sampler --- the
Diffusion-Policy formulation.
\item[Action chunking (sequence diffusion).] The diffused variable is a whole
$H$-step action sequence, not a single action. The network treats the chunk as a
unit, which lets it produce temporally consistent, multi-modal plans. At run time
\code{act} executes only the first \code{n\_exec} actions and re-plans --- a
receding-horizon scheme, not one-shot generation.
\end{description}

\subsection{Representation tricks that make the Gaussian prior appropriate}
DDPM denoises toward an $\mathcal N(0,I)$-scaled target, so the data must be put on
that scale (\file{train\_diffusion\_policy.py}):
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
\item[Angular (wrap-free) features.] Raw angles are replaced by
$[\sin q_1,\cos q_1,\sin q_2,\cos q_2]$ so the network never sees a $2\pi$
discontinuity in the conditioning (\code{\_state\_to\_features}).
\item[Min--max normalization to {$[-1,1]$}.] Velocities and the diffused
\emph{actions} are linearly rescaled to $[-1,1]$ (\code{\_norm\_action}); this
matches the unit scale the diffusion prior assumes and keeps the noise schedule
meaningful. The exact min/max are saved to \texttt{norm\_stats.json} and inverted
at inference (\code{denorm\_action}).
\item[History $+$ goal conditioning.] The context is the last $k$ feature vectors
concatenated with the per-trajectory goal feature, with left-padding at the start
of a trajectory and right-padding (repeat the last action) at the end.
\end{description}

\subsection{Training-loop tricks}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
\item[EMA weights as the inference model.] An exponential moving average of the
parameters (decay $0.999$) is maintained every step (\code{\_update\_ema}) and is
what \code{save\_checkpoint} stores as \code{model\_state}. The EMA copy is a
smoother estimate of the trained function; near the unstable upright equilibrium
this yields a more \emph{consistent} learned gain (less jitter), which is what
allows the policy to balance at all.
\item[Short, tuned noise schedule.] $T=100$ steps (not $\sim\!1000$) with a linear
$\beta$ from $3\!\times\!10^{-4}$ to $3\!\times\!10^{-2}$ keeps reverse sampling
cheap enough to run inside a control loop while still denoising cleanly
(\code{Scheduler}).
\item[Normalized continuous timestep.] The network is fed $t/T\in(0,1)$ rather
than the integer index (\code{t\_normalized = t / timesteps}), so the timestep
embedding sees a bounded, scale-free input. (The Transformer's sinusoidal
embedding multiplies this by $1000$ to recover useful frequencies.)
\item[Restricted $t$ range.] Training draws $t\sim\mathcal U\{1,\dots,T{-}1\}$
(\code{torch.randint(1, timesteps)}): $t=0$ is the clean sample (nothing to
denoise) and is skipped.
\item[Per-step cosine-annealing LR.] The LR follows a cosine schedule advanced
\emph{every batch}, with $T_{\max}=\text{epochs}\times|\text{loader}|$
(\code{\_lr\_scheduler.step()} inside the batch loop), rather than the constant or
per-epoch LR of a minimal trainer.
\item[Mixed-precision training.] On CUDA the forward/backward run under
\code{autocast} with a \code{GradScaler} --- a speed optimization that leaves the
algorithm unchanged.
\end{description}

\subsection{Inference / sampling tricks}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
\item[Deterministic sampling --- the headline control trick.] The reverse process
takes the DDPM posterior \emph{mean} at each step,
\begin{equation}
a_{t-1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta_t}}\,a_t
\;-\;\frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\;
\varepsilon_\theta(a_t,t,\text{cond}),
\end{equation}
and with \code{stochastic=False} \emph{omits} the ancestral noise term entirely.
Vanilla DDPM always injects $\sqrt{\beta_t}\,z$; here that injected torque noise
would perturb the command and topple the pendulum, so for the unstable upright
hold the posterior mean is used to drive the action variance toward zero.
\item[Fixed-large posterior variance.] When stochasticity \emph{is} wanted, the
added noise uses $\sigma_t = \sqrt{\beta_t}$ (the ``fixed-large'' variance), and
only for $t>1$ --- the final denoising step ($t=1$) is always noise-free
(\code{sample}).
\item[Receding-horizon execution.] \code{act} predicts the full horizon but
returns only the first \code{n\_exec} actions, so the policy re-conditions on fresh
observations frequently instead of committing to a whole open-loop chunk.
\end{description}

\subsection{Backbone tricks}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
\item[A shared \code{forward(a\_noisy, t, cond)} contract] lets the MLP and the
Transformer be swapped with a one-line change, and makes the choice
self-describing in the checkpoint.
\item[Transformer conditioning by context tokens.] Instead of concatenating $t$
and \code{cond} into the input, the Transformer \emph{prepends} them as two extra
tokens so every action token attends to them; only the $H$ action positions are
projected back to $n_u$. It uses pre-norm GELU encoder layers and small-init
learned positional embeddings.
\end{description}

\section{Interfaces and Data Contracts}
\label{sec:interfaces}

The stages communicate only through files, which keeps them decoupled
(Figure~\ref{fig:flow}). The contracts are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Stage 1 $\rightarrow$ 2:} a reference folder
$\{$\texttt{trajectory.csv}~$(N,4)$, \texttt{inputs.csv}~$(N,2)$,
\texttt{K\_matrix.npy}~$(N{-}1,2,4)$, \texttt{config.json}$\}$. The
\code{load\_reference} reader in Stage~2 mirrors the \code{save\_trajectory}
writer in Stage~1.
\item \textbf{Stage 2 $\rightarrow$ 4:} an HDF5 file of \texttt{traj\_*} groups,
each \texttt{states (T,4)} + \texttt{actions (T,2)} with $Q/R/x_{\text{goal}}$
attributes. Stage~4's \code{DiffusionPolicyDataset} reads exactly this layout.
\item \textbf{Stage 4 $\rightarrow$ 3 (inference):} a self-describing checkpoint
(\code{net\_kwargs}, EMA weights) plus \texttt{norm\_stats.json}. The
\code{build\_network}/\code{save\_checkpoint} pair is the single source of truth
that lets evaluation rebuild the model from Stage~3 classes.
\end{itemize}

\section{Known Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

The documentation in the modules and the project's working notes record several
honest caveats about the current controller:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Goal-conditioning is presently nominal.} Although the dataset tags
each demonstration with its $x_{\text{goal}}$ and the trainer appends a goal
feature, the learned policy can effectively ignore its goal input when the
demonstrations are dominated by a single goal --- the goal feature must actually
\emph{vary} across the dataset (multiple $x_g$ in the sweep) for conditioning to
take effect.
\item \textbf{Closed-loop behavioural-cloning fragility.} A policy that imitates
the TVLQR expert can still drift in closed loop due to covariate shift (the policy
visits states the expert rarely demonstrated). The DAgger noise, mirroring, and
goal-hold rollouts of Stage~2 are explicitly there to mitigate this, but they do
not eliminate it.
\item \textbf{The inverted hold needs a fast rate.} The unstable upright hold is
not stabilisable at the $20$\,Hz control rate; Stage~2 re-closes the hold loop at
the inner $dt_{\text{sim}}$, and a policy that only commands at $20$\,Hz cannot
exactly reproduce that fast feedback. Deterministic sampling
(\code{stochastic=False}) and the EMA weights are the model-side mitigations.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
The four modules form a clean, file-decoupled imitation-learning pipeline:
classical trajectory optimization and TVLQR build a provably-valid expert
(\file{kux\_generation.py}); closed-loop rollouts with symmetry, DAgger noise and
goal-hold coverage harvest demonstrations (\file{generate\_tvlqr\_dataset\_kux.py});
a conditional diffusion model with MLP/Transformer backbones provides an
expressive multi-modal action generator (\file{diffusion\_policy.py}); and a
self-describing trainer fits it by noise-prediction behavioural cloning
(\file{train\_diffusion\_policy.py}). The design choices throughout --- analytic
gradients, RK4-consistent TVLQR discretisation, the separation of control and
integration rates, EMA weights, and deterministic sampling for the unstable hold
--- are all driven by the difficulty of stabilising an underactuated,
torque-limited double pendulum at an unstable equilibrium.
